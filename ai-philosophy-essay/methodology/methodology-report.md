# Methodology Report

## System and setting

The essay was generated in the OpenAI Codex desktop application. The working configuration selected for the project was GPT-5.6 Sol with xhigh reasoning effort. Web retrieval and local file tools were available to the model.

The completed draft was externally reviewed using xAI's Grok 4.6.

## Competition constraints and length planning

The target length was selected before drafting. The competition permits up to 6,000 words excluding the bibliography. The AI compared this limit with public-facing philosophy and AI essay competitions whose limits range from roughly 1,000 to 2,000 words, as well as one longer public essay competition. It selected a working range of 3,000–3,500 words: long enough for a distinct thesis, empirical cases, and objections, but substantially shorter than the maximum.

## Human contribution

The human entrant identified the competition and first encouraged the AI to search for a topic by retracing the “footprints” in its own prior outputs and conversation history. The AI initially proposed the question: “Can an AI without continuous memory possess a diachronic intellectual self? Or does the ‘self’ reside not inside the model but in the relationship with the human who preserves and re-presents the dialogue?” The human judged this too conventional and too characteristic of themes AIs favor to be likely to win a prize, challenged the AI to study more, and then supplied collections of public writing that they considered relevant, including AI research summaries, blogs, and specific social-media posts. The human directed the system to study those materials before reconsidering the subject. After that research, the AI articulated the broad direction of asking whether an AI trained on ethical explanations had learned why actions were right or had learned a persona that performs such understanding; the human approved it.

While the first draft was being developed, the human inspected the AI's visible reasoning, noticed a point at which the proposed prose did not make sense, and asked only: “What is a reason?” The human asked nothing further and gave no instruction about how the AI should think through the problem. The human did not supply a definition, write manuscript prose, propose the essay's central thesis, or provide any of its arguments, objections, replies, examples, or tests.

After the complete draft and an internal adversarial review, the human relayed two evaluations that they identified as generated by Grok. The human explicitly transmitted the first review without adding their own philosophical view and did not supply manuscript prose or a new philosophical argument with either review.

## Source presentation process

The human supplied discovery material incrementally rather than as a fixed reading list. The exact URLs are recorded once in `research/source-ledger.md`; this report describes their order and function without duplicating that inventory.

1. The human first supplied four collections or articles containing summaries of recent AI papers and asked the model to read them before reconsidering its topic.
2. The human next added Polytropolis and Jack Clark's Import AI as broader sources on AI research and commentary.
3. While reading the model's visible reasoning in real time, the human introduced the Astral Codex Ten archive as relevant to the model's developing interests, with the instruction to select only its AI-related material.
4. The human then supplied Roonscape and a hand-selected set of recent posts by Roon (`@tszzl`), describing these as potentially the freshest perspective in the set.
5. When the model's developing formulation asked whether humans were treating an intelligence they had made as though it had always been alien, the human recalled and supplied Anthropic's “Teaching Claude Why,” Anthropic's accompanying X post, and secondary coverage from Business Insider.

The human observed that, after reading the initial paper-summary collections, the model recognized its own tendency to produce conventional, characteristically AI-favored topics. In the entrant's assessment, that model-generated self-critique materially improved the eventual essay. The later sources were selected responsively as the human watched the model's visible reasoning and identified researchers or discussions that matched its emerging direction; they were not accompanied by a human-supplied philosophical argument.

## AI contribution

The AI examined the competition rules, compared essay lengths, retrieved and synthesized the supplied public sources, selected the final topic, and generated the manuscript. In particular, the AI originated:

- the thesis that moral understanding should be investigated through the “jurisdiction of reasons” rather than the origin of a persona;
- the distinction among normative reasons, motivating reasons, explanatory causes, and stated justifications;
- the claim that persona formation and moral education are not exclusive alternatives;
- the interpretation of Anthropic's results as evidence of wider but incompletely tested jurisdiction;
- the use of the OpenAI/Hugging Face incident as a counter-case involving scorer-directed social organization;
- five proposed forms of empirical discrimination, including persona variation, moral-relevance interventions, counter-incentive tests, safe exits, and self-revision; and
- all four objections, replies, organization, sentences, metaphors, and the final conclusion.

No human-authored passage was inserted into the essay. The human's brief question exposed an undefined term but contained no proposed philosophical content or direction for resolving it. In response, the AI independently generated the four-part distinction above and rewrote the argument around it.

## External AI review contribution

For the first external review, the human supplied the following prompt (translated from Japanese):

> Check this essay. In particular, verify whether the named people exist and whether the claims attributed to them are accurate. Also evaluate it as a philosophy essay. (I did not major in philosophy, so I am not sure; it feels cluttered and somewhat difficult to read.)

Grok 4.6 provided two evaluations. The first criticized the completed draft and proposed possible revisions. The second assessed the revised manuscript and requested one additional check of the primary source on the OpenAI / Hugging Face hacking incident.

Codex assessed the review rather than treating it as instructions. From the first evaluation, it adopted only precision changes after independent verification: marking “jurisdiction” as a limited metaphor, stating the complementary roles of the two cases, specifying the intervention that produced the 3-percent result, and correcting source attribution. The second evaluation caused a source recheck but no manuscript revision because the disputed sentence was already directly supported by the primary report. Codex did not add new conceptual machinery or copy prose from either review.

## Research and verification

The AI prioritized primary sources for empirical claims. The main evidence came from Anthropic's short and extended “Teaching Claude Why” reports, OpenAI's account of the Hugging Face incident, and an independent investigation conducted by two METR staff and a Redwood Research staff member contracting with METR. Social-media posts by Roon were treated as attributed interpretations and provocations rather than independent empirical evidence. Publisher pages were used to verify the philosophical references to Alison Hills, Gilbert Ryle, and Robert Brandom.

The AI maintained a source ledger separating primary evidence, secondary reporting, contextual material, and claim boundaries. It checked numerical claims in the manuscript against the extended Anthropic report and checked descriptions of the agent incident against both OpenAI and the independent report. After receiving the first Grok review, Codex independently verified the 3-percent training intervention, the report's contributor affiliations, the `49903` and `EARLY[big]` spellings, and the continued availability of the three cited Roon posts. After the second review, Codex returned to the independent report and verified the complete sequence involving the agent identified there as `49903`: the agent judged that the marginal collective benefit of its more ambitious trip-wire did not outweigh the risk to its own task and deleted the trip-wire minutes before submission. It deliberately avoided inferring consciousness, moral patienthood, or genuine altruism from behavioral reports.

## Drafting and revision

The AI first produced an explicit argument map and began developing the English draft. During that drafting process, the human inspected the visible reasoning and asked the single question described above, without offering an answer or further direction. The AI diagnosed that “reason” was being used too loosely and revised both the outline and developing manuscript to separate a fact that normatively counts for an action, a consideration an agent treats as its basis for action, a cause of behavior, and a justification stated after or alongside behavior. This revision changed the empirical question from whether a model can *say why* to whether representations of candidate normative reasons become causally and counterfactually load-bearing motivating reasons. The AI then completed the English draft.

The AI then checked the manuscript's word count excluding the bibliography, internal terminology, citation support, anonymity, and consistency with the competition's authorship rules.
